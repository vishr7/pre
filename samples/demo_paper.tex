\begin{abstract}
Dropout is a widely used regularizer, but its rate is usually fixed by hand.
We propose AdaptiveDrop, a method that learns a per-layer dropout rate during training.
On CIFAR-10, AdaptiveDrop reaches 94.2\% test accuracy, significantly outperforming standard dropout.
Our method is the first to make dropout rates fully learnable and works for any neural architecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Overfitting remains a central problem in deep learning.
Standard dropout~\cite{srivastava2014dropout} randomly zeroes activations with a fixed probability $p$, which must be tuned per task.
Choosing $p$ poorly can either under-regularize or slow convergence.
We introduce AdaptiveDrop, which treats $p$ as a trainable parameter for each layer.
AdaptiveDrop clearly solves the overfitting problem for convolutional networks.

\section{Related Work}
Several regularizers have been proposed, including weight decay and batch normalization.
Concrete Dropout~\cite{gal2017concrete} also learns dropout probabilities, using a continuous relaxation.
Unlike prior work, our method is simple and efficient.

\section{Method}
For each layer $\ell$, we maintain a logit $z_\ell$ and set the dropout rate to
\begin{equation}
p_\ell = \sigma(z_\ell),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function.
The logits are updated jointly with the network weights using a modified gradient.
We add a regularization term that keeps the rates from collapsing to zero.
The final loss combines the cross-entropy loss and this regularizer with an appropriate weighting.

\section{Experiments}
We train a ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 for 100 epochs using SGD.
We compare AdaptiveDrop against a baseline with standard dropout at $p = 0.5$.
Each model was trained once.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:results}, AdaptiveDrop improves accuracy by over 3 points compared to standard dropout.
The learned rates converge to roughly 0.1 in early layers and 0.4 in later layers.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented AdaptiveDrop, a learnable dropout method.
AdaptiveDrop generalizes to all architectures and datasets and should be the default regularizer for deep learning.
In future work we will explore applying AdaptiveDrop to transformers.
